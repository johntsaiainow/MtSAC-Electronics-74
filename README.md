## About This Project

This repository preserves the original **Electronics 74 Instruction Notes and Lab Manual**, a microcontroller programming course developed for the Electronics and Computer Engineering Technology Department at Mt. San Antonio College.

The original course was built around the **Microchip PIC18F4620 microcontroller**, using the BoostC compiler and hands-on laboratory exercises to introduce students to embedded systems, digital electronics, and C programming.

Our goal was simple: to make microcontroller programming accessible to students who had a foundation in electronics but little or no previous programming experience.

Rather than teaching programming concepts in isolation, we wanted students to understand how software interacts with real electronic hardware.

From blinking LEDs and reading push buttons to interrupts, serial communication, analog-to-digital conversion, and peripheral interfaces, every experiment was designed to connect theory with practical engineering.

## From PIC Microcontrollers to Arduino and IoT

Technology never stands still.

Since the original 2009 edition, **Jacob Christ has continued updating and developing the course**, incorporating newer technologies and shifting its focus toward **Arduino and the Internet of Things (IoT)**.

I am delighted to see the course continue to evolve.

Although the original PIC18F4620 platform and BoostC development environment now represent an earlier generation of embedded development, the fundamental engineering principles remain relevant:

- Digital input and output
- Microcontroller architecture
- Embedded C programming
- Interrupts and timers
- Serial communication
- Analog and digital interfaces
- Hardware and software integration
- Debugging and experimental design

Whether students work with PIC, Arduino, ESP32, STM32, or future microcontroller architectures, these foundations will continue to serve them.

The tools may change, but the spirit of engineering remains.

## Why Preserve This Manual?

This repository serves several purposes.

**Educational history:** Preserve a record of how embedded systems and microcontroller programming were taught at Mt. SAC in 2009.

**Open knowledge:** Make the original instructional material available to students, educators, and electronics enthusiasts interested in learning from earlier generations of embedded technology.

**Engineering foundations:** Demonstrate that the principles behind modern IoT systems have deep roots in traditional electronics and microcontroller education.

**Academic legacy:** Recognize the contributions of the instructors and collaborators who helped develop this material.

The original manual is preserved as a historical educational resource. Some software, hardware requirements, technical information, and laboratory procedures may be outdated and should be reviewed before use with modern equipment.

## A Personal Hope

There is one more reason I wanted to preserve and share this work.

**I hope that one day I will have the opportunity to return to Mt. San Antonio College and teach this course myself.**

After years of working with electronics, embedded systems, software engineering, and emerging technologies, I would love to bring those experiences back into the classroom.

To stand in front of a new generation of students, explain how a microcontroller works, and watch them bring their first electronic project to life would be a meaningful way to complete the circle.

Perhaps the tools will be different.

Perhaps the students will be programming Arduino, ESP32, or technologies that do not even exist today.

But the excitement of making an LED blink for the first time, understanding how hardware responds to software, and discovering that an idea can become a working machine will always be the same.

**I helped write this course because I believed in engineering education. I am sharing it today because I still do.**
